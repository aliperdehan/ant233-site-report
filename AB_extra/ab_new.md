## @simek1997

**Citation:**

[[fullcite:simek1997]]

**Summary:**

They present the first recent analysis of Krapina's 1,191 stone artefacts, comparing Gorjanović-Kramberger's stratigraphic levels. The assemblage is a scraper-rich Charentian Mousterian made largely from local Krapinica River cobbles, with tuffs and silicified tuffs making up 65% and chert 23%. Typology stays stable across levels, but raw material choice and blank production shift: volcanic tuff dominates early levels, higher-quality silicified tuffs and cherts later ones, and Levallois flaking is more frequent earlier.

**Evaluation:**

Published in a respected, peer-reviewed journal and the first stratigraphic reanalysis of the Krapina lithics. Only retouched tools were recorded by level, and no level reaches the minimum tool count for typological analysis, so the trends are tentative. It will be used for the material culture section, showing local sourcing, technological flexibility, and the tool kit that could have made the cut marks debated by @russell1987 and @trinkaus1985.

## @miracle2011

**Citation:**

[[fullcite:miracle2011]]

**Summary:**

He analyses the sex and size of the cave bears, the most numerous vertebrate at Krapina. The assemblage is heavily male (63.6--100% depending on the element), with no evidence of hominin involvement in the bear accumulation (only 2.2% of the total assemblage is cut-marked). Krapina's male bears are smaller than those from Divje babe I (MIS 3 and MIS 5a--d), which he reads as an adaptation to a full interglacial climate. He concludes that the sequence is restricted to MIS 5e and accumulated over thousands, not tens of thousands, of years.

**Evaluation:**

Published in a peer-reviewed volume by the zooarchaeologist who wrote the Krapina faunal monograph. Body size gives climate and chronology evidence independent of the ESR dates in @rink1995, though it assumes the bear assemblage is homogeneous, which he admits is difficult to test. It will support the climate and stratigraphy sections. It is a cave bear study, so it rules out Neanderthal bear hunting, but its refined chronology does speak to rapid Neanderthal behavioral shifts.
